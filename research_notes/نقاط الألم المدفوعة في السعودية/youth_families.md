## Students: admission tests (Qudurat/Tahsili), graduation projects, assignments, English tests, scholarships. What do they pay for?

### Takeaway
Families already pay for tutoring at prices the government sets (SR50–100 per class), and students pay small sums ($25–50) to freelancers for academic presentations. Qudurat/Tahsili prep is a crowded, low-quality, low-price market: a full Tahsili course sells for about $92 on Gumroad. The clearest gap a solo designer can fill ethically is presentation design for theses and graduation projects, plus coaching on structure and formatting. Ghost-writing is a clear integrity violation, and these notes recommend against it.

### Cited Findings
- The Ministry of Education allows paid private evening lessons. At schools they cost SR50 per class (elementary), SR60 (middle) and SR70 (high school). At home they cost SR80 (elementary) and SR100 (middle and high). The article is undated — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-gives-green-light-for-private-evening-lessons-in-schools-and-homes-1.95798563)
- Gulf parents describe private tutoring as a habit that drains household budgets, with prices rising near exams. This is a 2022 regional source (Qatar-based paper), so it is only indirect evidence for Saudi Arabia — [Al-Sharq](https://al-sharq.com/article/08/05/2022/أولياء-أمور-لـ-الشرق-نحتاج-حلولا-جذرية-للقضاء-على-ظاهرة-الدروس-الخصوصية)
- A 2022 academic paper says private tutoring at Saudi school and university level is under-researched, so no reliable national spending figure exists — [Complexity/DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/ea47b5eea7534cf98b8e3291efcbff91)
- "دورة التحصيلي 2025" (full Tahsili course, all subjects) sells for $92 on Gumroad. This shows students pay for digital prep products — [Gumroad](https://edu1ink.gumroad.com/l/vpunum)
- Many Qudurat apps (قياس القدرات، درع التفوق) are free with in-app purchases, and local institutes advertise Tahsili programmes on Snapchat, e.g. one starting Feb 2026 — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1533551970); [Snapchat](https://www.snapchat.com/@qdrat162). The earlier report found one app with 81k+ downloads rated 2.9 — [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/dr-lhrf/1643227111)
- Mostaql requests: a student wants a PowerPoint for a master's thesis (budget $25–50, 2-day delivery, with graphics from the thesis). Another wants a 10+ slide defence deck covering introduction, problem, questions, objectives and results (similar budget, 1-day delivery) — [Mostaql 1283703](https://mostaql.com/go/1283703); [Mostaql 1256168](https://mostaql.com/go/1256168)
- STEP (Qiyas English test) fees are about SR100 (general) to SR150 (late) inside the Kingdom and up to SR200 abroad. This comes from a third-party guide dated July 2026, so it is unverified — [open-exam-prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sa-etec-step-english/introduction/registration-test-day-and-score-interpretation)
- STEP scores are used as admission gates. KFUPM early admission requires STEP 55+ or an equivalent TOEFL/IELTS score — [KFUPM](https://admissions.kfupm.edu.sa/en/new-admission/admission's-tracks). SEU accepts STEP or IELTS — [SEU](https://seu.edu.sa/en/directorates/common-first-year-directorate/english-course-faqs). PSU lists Academic IELTS 4 as an entry option — [PSU](https://www.psu.edu.sa/pdf/Admissions%20Flyer%20ENG.pdf)

### Inferences
- Willingness to pay is proven but the price ceiling is low. The pattern is SR50–100 per tutoring hour, about $92 for a full course, and $25–50 for a thesis deck. Products should be priced at SR29–199, or sold to parents as bundles.
- **Idea 1 – "عرض المناقشة" (defence deck studio):** Arabic RTL templates for graduation projects and theses (Gumroad/Salla) plus a done-for-you design service at a fixed SR199–499. This is ethical because the student supplies the content. It also fits the earlier report's RTL-template opportunity.
- **Idea 2 – STEP-focused micro-course and question-strategy app:** STEP prep has less competition than IELTS and is tied to Saudi admission and employment thresholds. Copyright risk: do not copy Qiyas items.
- Ethical line: formatting, proofreading, citation help, statistics tutoring and presentation design are acceptable. Writing assignments or graduation projects for students is a violation that university guidelines (e.g. KSU's graduation-project guide) prohibit — [KSU guide](https://education.ksu.edu.sa/sites/education.ksu.edu.sa/files/users/user311/599Guideline.pdf).

### Gaps
- No credible figure for Saudi household spending on Qudurat/Tahsili prep, and no current price list from major centres.
- No 2025–2026 data on English requirements for the Custodian of the Two Holy Mosques scholarship programme or on IELTS demand volumes in KSA.
- No sample of Khamsat/Mostaql "حل واجبات" listings was retrieved, so the scale of the assignment-help grey market is unverified.

## Job seekers: youth and women unemployment, CV/interview, Tamheer/co-op, first-job transition. What paid help exists?

### Takeaway
Unemployment is concentrated among young Saudi women: 20.4% for ages 15–24 in Q1 2026, against 13.8% for young men. The overall Saudi rate is about 6.4–6.5%. Paid help exists but is commoditised: ATS-compliant CVs on Khamsat start at $5. Free government channels (Tamheer pays SR2,000–3,000 a month; Jadarat; Taqat) cap what anyone can charge for job boards. Room remains for structured coaching for the first-job transition, paid for by young people or their parents.

### Cited Findings
- Q1 2026: Saudi unemployment was 6.4%. Saudi women aged 15–24 were at 20.4% (down 2 points) and Saudi men aged 15–24 at 13.8% (down 1.2 points) — [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/gcc/saudi-unemployment-falls-to-64-as-womens-jobless-rate-drops-to-9-qs8rzh9a); [Gulf News via earlier report](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-unemployment-falls-to-64-in-q1-2026-1.500592445)
- Q2 2026: the Saudi rate was 6.5% and the overall rate (all nationalities) 3.0%. Sources conflict on the women's rate: Trading Economics reports 7.6% (apparently all women), while another report cites 10.3% for Saudi women from the 2025 annual data. No Q2 youth breakdown was found — [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/saudi-arabias-unemployment-rate-drops-to-6.5-in-q2-2026-1459142); [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/saudi-arabia/unemployment-rate/news/588098)
- Tamheer (HRDF) pays SR2,000 a month to diploma holders and SR3,000 to bachelor's holders for 3–6 months. Eligibility requires 6+ months of unemployment or no GOSI registration and no prior use of the programme. The service is free. These are 2022–2023 figures and current amounts are unverified — [Saudipedia](https://saudipedia.com/en/on-the-job-training-program-%22tamheer%22); [HRDF](https://hrdf.org.sa/en/products-and-services/programs/individuals/training/graduate-development/apply); [Al-Fanar](https://al-fanarmedia.org/2022/11/saudi-arabias-tamheer-training-scheme/)
- Misk ran a Tamheer cohort from 3 Aug 2025 to 30 Apr 2026, with applications closing 30 Sep 2025 — [Misk](https://hub.misk.org.sa/programs/skills/misk-graduate-traineeship-program-tamheer/)
- Khamsat has many listings for ATS-compliant Arabic/English CVs. One costs $5 base, $10 for bilingual and $20 for an "ATS test" add-on, and some sellers say they target the Saudi and Egyptian markets — [Khamsat 4220062](https://khamsat.com/writing/resume-writing/4220062-%D9%83%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D8%B3%D9%8A%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%B0%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D8%AD%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%AA%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%81%D9%82%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B9-ats); [Khamsat 3878339](https://khamsat.com/writing/resume-writing/3878339-%D9%83%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%A8%D8%A9-%D9%88%D8%AA%D8%B5%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%85-%D8%B3%D9%8A%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%B0%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D8%AD%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%AA%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%81%D9%82%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B9-ats)
- StylingCV (a commercial tool with a 2026 Saudi CV guide) targets the same demand and claims ATS adoption is growing in Saudi public and private employers. This is a vendor claim — [StylingCV](https://stylingcv.com/blog/دليل-كتابة-سيرة-ذاتية-احترافية-2026-نماذج-pdf/)

### Inferences
- A standalone CV is a race to the bottom at $5. The pain is the whole transition: knowing which programme to apply to (Tamheer, co-op, Hadaf support), building a LinkedIn profile, preparing for interviews (often in English), and handling the first-salary negotiation.
- **Idea 1 – "أول وظيفة" kit plus cohort:** a paid digital guide/Notion system (SR79–149) covering Tamheer/Jadarat/Taqat steps, ATS templates and a bank of Saudi interview questions. Upsell: a 4-week online cohort for women graduates (SR499–999), run online to fit the large female 15–24 unemployment pool.
- **Idea 2 – mock interviews delivered by Saudi HR professionals,** marketed to parents ("هدية التخرج"). The service is light, with no licence beyond a commercial registration or freelance document (unverified).

### Gaps
- No Q2 2026 youth breakdown, and the women's figure is unresolved (7.6% vs 10.3%).
- No data on Saudis' willingness to pay for career coaching beyond Khamsat pricing, and no sales volume per Khamsat listing.
- Current Tamheer stipend amounts for 2025–2026 are not confirmed.

## Newly married: wedding costs, mahr, housing setup, furniture, home ownership (Sakani)

### Takeaway
Marriage is a heavy financial shock for young men. Mahr is commonly SR20k–100k, a full wedding costs about SR150k–300k (commercial estimate), and loans are common: interest-free marriage loans go up to SR60k. Home ownership is improving (66.24% at end-2025, target 70% by 2030), but setting up a first home still falls on newlyweds. Leaner weddings are a visible trend: planners report average spend down about 25%. That favours budgeting tools, DIY digital products and small-wedding packages over luxury services.

### Cited Findings
- Arab News, Mar 2026 (snippet): marriage brings "a heavy dose of financial stress" for many young Saudis. A bride said "Fifty thousand riyals ($13,330) used to mean something different… Now, it disappears so fast." Leaders promote smaller weddings, and experts say lower mahr alone won't fix housing and job pressures. A lawyer cites interest-free marriage loans of up to SR60,000 — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2638160)
- Semafor opinion piece, Jul 2026: Saudi courts received 1.6M debt-collection requests in 2025 worth SR165bn+. The author argues young men carry a disproportionate share of financial obligations. This is opinion, not wedding-specific — [Semafor](https://semafor.com/article/07/21/2026/saudi-men-are-drowning-in-debt)
- AFP/Tribune: Saudi wedding planners report average spend down about 25% in a year as small weddings spread. The source notes that statistics on frugal weddings are scarce — [Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/1887487/saudi-arabia-small-weddings-new-big-thing). A 2019 Ajel report describes home weddings used to cut costs — [Ajel](https://ajel.sa/local/s2fdcv)
- A commercial calculator estimates total costs at SR150k–300k and mahr at SR20k–100k, covering venue, catering, the gold set (شبكة) and honeymoon. This is not a survey, so it is unverified — [hijri-calendars](https://hijri-calendars.com/blog/wedding-cost-calculator-saudi-guide)
- Older context: 2017 reports of mahr above SR250k, and a SR50k cap that could not be verified — [Noon Post](https://www.noonpost.com/20169/amp/)
- Saudi home ownership was 66.24% at end-2025, above the 65% annual target, and 65.4% in 2024. The target is 70% by 2030 — [Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/node/5298787); [Gulf Construction](https://gulfconstructiononline.com/ArticleTA/467182)
- Sakani: 48,000+ families moved into a first home in H1 2025, and subsidised mortgage contracts rose 63% year on year — [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1832782). Knight Frank estimates 115k new homes a year are needed and treats marriage and household formation as the main demand driver — [Arab News/Knight Frank](https://arab.news/rxkp7)

### Inferences
- Pain sequence: mahr/شبكة, then the wedding night (venue, separate men's and women's sections), then furnishing the apartment and appliances, then a mortgage or rent. Each stage is paid for, and each is planned today on WhatsApp and in notebooks.
- **Idea 1 – "جهازي" newlywed home-setup planner:** Arabic budget and checklist (Notion/Sheets/PDF, SR49–99) for furnishing a first apartment, covering room-by-room lists, appliance comparisons and a BNPL/installment tracker (Tabby/Tamara are widely used: unverified). Later: affiliate links to furniture stores. This complements the earlier report's wedding-planner and e-invitation ideas.
- **Idea 2 – remote "first home" interior styling packages** for Sakani/off-plan apartment buyers: a fixed-price moodboard plus shopping list (SR499–1,500). This links to the 48k-families-per-half-year flow. No licence needed for design advice (verify if construction is involved).

### Gaps
- No official 2024–2026 survey of average wedding or mahr cost in KSA. All figures are anecdotal or commercial.
- No data found on spending to furnish a newlywed home, or on newlyweds' rent burden.
- The current status and terms of the SR60k marriage loan (Social Development Bank) were not verified directly.

## Young families: childcare and nurseries, school choice and fees, kids' activities, parenting support, domestic workers

### Takeaway
Working parents pay heavily for childcare. Riyadh nurseries reportedly cost SR27k a year (local) to SR50k (international) (snippet). Recruiting a domestic worker costs SR5,900–14,700 under the Jan 2024 caps, and a 2018–19 survey found 56% of working mothers rely on nannies or domestic workers, spending about one-fifth of their income. The Qurrah subsidy covers at most 50% / SR1,600 per child, but take-up was low because of eligibility and preferences. School fees are regulated but opaque, and comparison sites (mdares.ai) are emerging. Parents also struggle with discovery and quality signals (nursery reviews complain of high fees, poor ventilation and rigid payment terms).

### Cited Findings
- Qurrah (HRDF): covers up to 50% of a nursery booking, capped at SAR 1,600 per child under 6, for eligible private-sector working mothers. 2025 registration is listed — [HRDF](https://www.hrdf.org.sa/en/programs/individuals/enable/childcare-support-for-working-women/)
- 2018–19 survey of 2,000 women (Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam): about 25% were eligible for Qurrah and about 15% used it. 56% of working mothers rely on domestic workers or nannies and spend about one-fifth of their income on them. Low take-up was attributed to preferences and eligibility, not lack of awareness — [BU GDP Center](https://www.bu.edu/gdp/2019/09/17/if-women-receive-more-childcare-support-will-they-work/)
- HRSD H1 2026 report on child-hosting centres: Riyadh 513 (about 26.3% of the national total, which implies roughly 1,950 centres nationally — inference), Eastern Province 498, Makkah 393 — [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/local/na/2249448)
- The Labour Law lets HRSD require employers with 100+ female workers in one city to build or contract a nursery — [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/local/na/2249448). The Council of Ministers directed MoE/HRSD to set rules for workplace nurseries in large institutions, timeline unconfirmed — [People Matters](https://me.peoplemattersglobal.com/news/economy-policy/saudi-arabia-pushes-workplace-nurseries-to-boost-womens-workforce-participation-49741)
- HRDF supported 200+ private childcare facilities (date not confirmed) — [Ajel English](https://english.ajel.sa/news/hrdf-provide-support-for-more-than-200-private-childcare-facilities)
- Riyadh nursery fees are about SAR 27,000 a year (local) and SAR 50,000 (international), and childcare costs deter women from working (snippet; page fetch failed) — [Argaam Weekend](https://weekend.argaam.com/76/en/Childcare_Gulf_Mideast)
- Reviews of Riyadh nurseries cite high fees, mediocre care, cramped space, poor ventilation and inflexible payment policies — [magicpin](https://magicpin.com/saudi-arabia/Riyadh/Al-Olaya/Education//store/2284280)
- Domestic worker recruitment caps from Jan 2024: Philippines SR14,700, Sri Lanka SR13,800, Bangladesh SR11,750, Kenya SR9,000, Uganda SR8,300, Ethiopia SR5,900, plus VAT. They are enforced through Musaned — [Arab News](https://arabnews.com/node/2444711/saudi-arabia); [Gulf News – Musaned fees](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-details-of-fees-for-hiring-domestic-workers-on-musaned-platform-1.101329175)
- School fees: private schools must register fees, and any change, on the National Education Portal a year ahead, and mid-year increases are illegal — [Arab News](https://arabnews.com/node/2253266). A Jeddah Chamber committee expected 60% of private schools to raise fees (undated) — [EFG Hermes One](https://www.efghermesone.com/News/Index/chamber-of-commerce-expects-60-of-private-schools-to-raise-tuition-fees). An op-ed says parents may pay up to a third of their annual salary on school fees (undated, opinion) — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/opinion/op-eds/saudi-arabia-needs-school-fees-reform-1.64603298)
- Riyadh school fees start at about SAR 2,430 a year (cheapest) and rise much higher at international schools, with extra costs for registration, transport, uniforms, books and activities — [mdares.ai](https://mdares.ai/sa-en/school-fees/riyadh)

### Inferences
- Pains with clear spending: (a) finding a trustworthy nursery near home or work, with real reviews, actual fees and Qurrah eligibility; (b) the total cost of schooling beyond tuition; (c) managing a domestic worker (contracts, schedules, Arabic/English instruction cards, onboarding); (d) after-school and weekend activities, for which no data was found.
- **Idea 1 – nursery and kids-activity directory with honest fee tables and a Qurrah eligibility checker,** for Riyadh first (513 centres is a manageable listing set). Revenue: featured listings and lead fees from nurseries, and paid "verified" badges. No licence needed for a directory. Note that mdares.ai already covers schools, so focus on nurseries (ages 0–6) and activities.
- **Idea 2 – "إدارة العاملة المنزلية" digital kit:** bilingual or trilingual (Arabic/English/Tagalog/Amharic) house-rules cards, daily schedules, child-care instructions and an onboarding checklist (SR39–79), with a later upsell to a simple app. It targets the 56% of working mothers who rely on domestic workers. It must respect labour-rights rules (Musaned contracts), and wording that suggests control over a worker's passport or movement must be avoided.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 national survey of nursery fees, waitlists or shortages, and no current Qurrah take-up figure.
- No data on spending for kids' activities or parenting courses, and no Reddit r/saudiarabia threads were retrieved (search returned none).
- No current (2025–2026) data on monthly domestic worker salaries or hourly maid services (e.g. Musaned "rental" services).
