# Saudi Market Gaps: Health & Care, and Education & Careers (as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: research by web search (English and Arabic), about 17 queries. App-store listings, LinkedIn profiles, consultancy guides and market-research press releases are weak evidence and are labeled that way. The brief already established three things, so they were not re-researched: no Saudi elderly-caregiver marketplace app was found; people aged 60+ are about 4.8% of the population (GASTAT); telehealth and mental-health apps already exist.

## Q1. What are the biggest unsolved customer pains (2025–2026)?

### Takeaway
In health, the biggest pains are chronic disease (diabetes and obesity), family caregivers carrying dementia and elderly care alone, a large untreated mental-health burden, and low physical activity among women and children. In careers, unemployment is concentrated among young Saudi women, and the bridge from university to a first job is weak (co-op placements, interview skills, CVs that pass ATS screening).

### Cited Findings
**Health & care**
- Diabetes prevalence sources disagree. One market report says about 18.7% of Saudi adults (2025), with obesity above 24%. Another, citing the IDF, says 23.1% of adults in 2024, about 5.3M people. A third source gives 18.7% as the 2021 IDF figure for ages 20–79. All of these are market-research sources, not primary data, so check the IDF Atlas. — [IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/saudi-arabia-diabetes-market); [IMARC digital diabetes](https://www.imarcgroup.com/saudi-arabia-digital-diabetes-management-market); [GlobeNewswire/R&M](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/09/26/3157074/28124/en/Saudi-Arabia-Digital-Health-Market-Trends-Analysis-Report-2025-2033-Vision-2030-Telemedicine-and-AI-Drive-Transformation-Amid-Rising-Chronic-Disease-and-Remote-Care-Demand.html)
- Saudi digital diabetes management market: USD 144.78M in 2024 (market-research estimate). — [IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/saudi-arabia-digital-diabetes-management-market)
- Saudi digital health market: USD 2.5B in 2024, projected USD 16.94B by 2033 (vendor forecast, weak evidence). — [BusinessWire/ResearchAndMarkets](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20251118966527/en/Saudi-Arabias-Digital-Health-Market-to-Grow-from-%242.5-Billion-in-2024-to-%2416.94-Billion-by-2033---Telemedicine-and-AI-Propel-Saudi-Arabias-Healthcare-Digitalization---ResearchAndMarkets.com)
- Dementia: about 130,000 people with Alzheimer's, a widely cited estimate. A 2024 review notes there are no published national prevalence studies. Women provide care for more than two-thirds of patients. A 2023 study of 148 Saudi caregivers found moderate-to-high burden and unmet needs for services. — [PMC9957410](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9957410/); [PMC11997542 (Jeddah needs study)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11997542/); [Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2023.1167856/full)
- Nurses in Jeddah (2024 study) said people with dementia get too little family support and public awareness is low. — [PMC11100391](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11100391)
- Mental health: the Saudi National Mental Health Survey (2019, n=4,004, ages 15–65) found 34.2% lifetime prevalence, about 40% among young people. Sources give 80% or 83% as the share of severe cases that do not seek treatment (unresolved). The most common disorders are separation anxiety (11.9%), ADHD (7.9%) and major depression (6%). — [KFSHRC](https://www.kfshrc.edu.sa/en/home/news/9697); [Saudipedia](https://saudipedia.com/en/article/2271/government-and-politics/health/mental-health-in-saudi-arabia); [Saudipedia disorders](https://saudipedia.com/en/what-are-the-most-common-mental-disorders-in-saudi-arabia)
- Physical activity (GASTAT 2024 bulletin): 58.5% of adults reach 150 minutes per week. Only 18.7% of children aged 5–17 meet the 60-minutes-a-day guideline, and Riyadh is lowest at 14.7%. The bulletin also reports a gender gap (23.2% of men vs 14% of women on one guideline measure). — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-585-of-adults-active-but-youth-sedentary-habits-on-the-rise-1.1733140777697). The older 2022 GASTAT survey (30-minute threshold, not comparable) gave 48.2% overall, 38.3% for females. — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2128576)
- The public MOH home health care programme covers chronic follow-up, IV therapy, palliative care and physiotherapy at MOH hospitals and is not limited to the elderly. In 2019 it ran 45 teams at 35 hospitals. — [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/awarenessplateform/patientsrights/pages/homehealthcare.aspx); [MOH 2019 news](https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/Ministry/MediaCenter/News/Pages/News-2019-04-01-001.aspx)
- Families' current route to elderly companionship is often hiring a domestic worker through the Musaned platform via licensed recruitment offices, not a care service. HRSD offers an elderly privilege card in the "Afrad" app and online residential-care requests. — [Al Watan](https://www.alwatan.com.sa/article/1168173)

**Education & careers**
- GASTAT Q1 2026: Saudi unemployment 6.4% (men 4.9%, women 9%). Saudi women aged 15–24 are at 20.4%. Saudi labour-force participation is 49%. The 7% Vision 2030 target has been reached. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-unemployment-falls-to-64-in-q1-2026-1.500592445); [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/gcc/saudi-unemployment-falls-to-64-as-womens-jobless-rate-drops-to-9-qs8rzh9a); [Arab News PK (overall 3.1%)](https://www.arabnews.pk/business/saudi-arabias-unemployment-rate-falls-to-31-gastat-2649072)
- University co-op training is a compulsory, admin-heavy matching problem. Taawoni was founded to digitize it (see Q2). — [Waya](https://waya.media/saudi-edtech-taawoni-raises-usd-1-6m-to-streamline-training-programs/)
- Qudurat and Tahsili (NCA/ETEC university-entry tests) prep is served by many small apps with low ratings. One app shows 81K+ downloads and a 2.9 rating. — [mwm.ai listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/dr-lhrf/1643227111); [App Store "Qodorat & More"](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/id6756188408)

### Inferences
- Health pains are concentrated in long-running, home-based problems (chronic disease, dementia and elderly care, rehab, activity) rather than one-off consultations, which telehealth already covers.
- Careers pains are concentrated among young women and new graduates. That points to career-readiness products (interviews, CVs, internships) rather than another job board, since Jadarat and Bayt-type boards already exist.

### Gaps
- No official 2025–2026 national obesity or diabetes figure from MOH or a national survey was found. Only conflicting market-research numbers turned up.
- No data was found on the home-nursing workforce or shortages.
- No official Qiyas (Qudurat/Tahsili) annual test-taker count was found. Check NCA/ETEC statistics.

## Q2. Which Saudi/GCC players already serve these pains, and how strong is competition?

### Takeaway
Telehealth, mental health, weight loss, K-12 edtech and government employment platforms are crowded or state-dominated. Medical home care is attracting large funding but is capital-heavy. Non-medical elderly companion care and family-caregiver tools, digital (app-based) physiotherapy, AI interview and CV prep, and high-quality Qudurat/Tahsili prep have only weak, small or hobby-level competitors.

### Cited Findings
**Health & care players**
- **Baraya Extended Care** (Riyadh, founded 2023; long-term care, rehab, home health): Series B, total $124M per CB Insights. This is an aggregator figure and is unverified. — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/baraya-extended-care)
- **Braya Medical Care** (home healthcare): Series B led by Al-Fozan Group, amount and date undisclosed. — [MENA Startup Digest](https://menastartupdigest.com/?p=8258)
- **WTDcare** (home healthcare and non-emergency medical transport): pre-seed led by Riyadh Angel Investors, announced at Global Health Exhibition, Oct 2025. Amount undisclosed. — [Bridge MENA](https://bridgemena.com/news/wtdcare-raises-pre-seed-funding-round)
- **TruDoc** (UAE, hospital-at-home): $15M pre-Series B in Mar 2026 to scale in the UAE and KSA. — [Enterprise AM](https://enterpriseam.com/uae/2026/03/09/al-nahyan-and-al-ketbi-families-anchored-trudoc-healthcares-usd-15-mn-funding-round/)
- **Healtec** (Saudi): raised $12M in its first round. The snippet did not say what the company does. — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/emea/saudis-healtec-raises-12m-inaugural-funding-round)
- **PhysioHome / Ameen / Tifool** (founded 2021): home and center-based rehab in 9 cities. Pre-seed in 2022 and seed in Feb 2024 from angels and accelerators. This is a home-visit model, not app-based exercise therapy. — [Descubre](https://www.descubre.vc/physiohome); [Sharikat Mubasher](https://sharikatmubasher.com/news/article/21372050)
- **Physiowell**: a Riyadh clinic that plans telehealth and AI assessment tools for 2026–2030 (plans, not product). PhysioMantra appears only in a job ad and is unverified. — [Sharikat Mubasher](https://sharikatmubasher.com/news/article/21372050); [MantraCare job](https://mantra.care/careers/physiotherapist-job-in-saudi-arabia/)
- **Kilow** (Riyadh, AI weight loss with GLP-1 prescribing by licensed physicians, Arabic-only, KSA-only): $2.5M seed in May 2025, led by Sanabil Venture Studio with Stryber. It has added a marketplace for labs, supplements and fitness products. — [Bridge MENA](https://bridgemena.com/news/saudis-kilow-raises-usd-2-5m-to-enhance-its-ai-powered-digital-weight-loss-platform); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/business%2Fmarkets%2Fstruggling-to-lose-weight-this-new-saudi-mobile-app-might-be-the-answer-1.500095612)
- **Labayh** (mental health teletherapy, MOH-accredited): seed led by 500 Global (2021). It reports about 1.2M users, self-reported. A 2021 profile put sessions at about SAR 100–200. CB Insights' "$10K raised" is implausible. — [Cairoscene](https://cairoscene.com/Business/How-Saudi-Startup-Labayh-Tackles-Stigmas-Around-Mental-Health-in-MENA); [Al Maktoum Initiatives](https://almaktouminitiatives.org/en/middle-east-exchange/story/suffer-no-more-saudi-app-delivers-mental-health-therapy-from-qualified-psychologists); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/labayh/financials)
- **Elderly companion or caregiver apps**: an Arabic search found no Saudi app for a home companion (مرافق منزلي). It found only "Awlawiya" (a social-services app for seniors with unclear features), HRSD digital services, and Musaned domestic-worker recruitment. — [Al Watan](https://www.alwatan.com.sa/article/1168173); [mwm.ai Awlawiya](https://mwm.ai/apps/awlawiya/1435000697)
- **Regulation**: the MOH Sandbox for digital health and AI testing launched in Aug 2024. — [GlobeNewswire/R&M](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/09/26/3157074/28124/en/Saudi-Arabia-Digital-Health-Market-Trends-Analysis-Report-2025-2033-Vision-2030-Telemedicine-and-AI-Drive-Transformation-Amid-Rising-Chronic-Disease-and-Remote-Care-Demand.html)

**Health competition summary (my assessment):** telehealth STRONG; mental-health teletherapy MODERATE–STRONG; medical home care MODERATE and well funded; GLP-1 and weight loss MODERATE (Kilow); app-based physio and MSK WEAK; non-medical elderly companion care and caregiver-support tools NONE FOUND to WEAK; family caregiver training for dementia NONE FOUND.

**Education & careers players**
- **Noon Academy**: $41M Series B (Nov 2023, co-led by Wa'ed Ventures and Raed Ventures). Reported totals are $62M raised and 16M+ learners (undated). — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/ys-gulf/saudis-edtech-startup-noon-funding-series-b); [ArabFounders](https://arabfounders.net/en/noon-academy-saudi-edtech-rise/embed/)
- **Classera**: a $40M Series A led by Sanabil is reported. Date and details are unverified. — [Rasmal](https://www.rasmal.com/edtech-startup-classera-raises-series-a-of-40-million-led-by-sanabil)
- **Taawoni** (co-op training management, B2B for universities and employers): $1.6M in May 2025 led by M Capital. It reports 14 institutions, 800+ businesses and 34,000+ trainees. — [Waya](https://waya.media/saudi-edtech-taawoni-raises-usd-1-6m-to-streamline-training-programs/); [Bridge MENA](https://bridgemena.com/news/taawoni-closes-1-6m-funding-round-to-accelerate-digital-transformation-in-co-op-and-professional-training)
- **Jadarat** (the unified national employment platform, run by HRDF): its pilot connected 114K job seekers with 48K organisations, and it listed 70K+ jobs at its 2024 launch. **Doroob** is the HRDF e-training platform. No 2025 user figures were found for either. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-launches-jadarat-employment-platform-with-over-70000-job-openings-1.103871823); [HRDF](https://www.hrdf.org.sa/en/media-center/news/general/droob-users/)
- **Tuwaiq Academy** (state-backed tech bootcamps): its CEO claims 30K+ graduates and over 75% employment. It runs a Google Cloud 3,000 Developers initiative (2025) and a Misk partnership. — [Arab News](https://arabnews.com/node/2580099/saudi-arabia); [Sharikat Mubasher](https://en.sharikatmubasher.com/media-hub/news/21496131/tuwaiq-academy-google-cloud-launch-3000-developers-initiative?lang=en)
- **AI CV and interview tools (all small)**: Astr (astrsa.com, an AI CV builder; 15K+ users claimed on the founder's LinkedIn); ATScv (atscv.co, an Arabic-first CV builder that scores CVs against Saudi ATS; Kanz AI Hackathon 2026 project); Intelliview (Arabic/English mock interviews, a Google Gemini competition project with unclear status). All are unverified and none is funded. — [LinkedIn Moath Alothman](https://sa.linkedin.com/in/moathalothman); [LinkedIn Ali Allaghabi](https://sa.linkedin.com/in/aliallaghabi); [Google AI competition](https://ai.google.dev/competition/projects/intelliview)
- **Qudurat/Tahsili prep**: a fragmented field of small apps (QudoratPlus "Qodorat & More" freemium, GPA Pro, "Qiyasak" simulator, "10 in 10 Qudurat", Ula, Dar Al Harf with 81K+ downloads). None has visible funding. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/id6756188408); [GPA Pro](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6782892565); [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/app/6470659721)

**Education competition summary (my assessment):** K-12 tutoring and LMS STRONG (Noon, Classera); government job matching and training STRONG and free (Jadarat, Doroob, Tamheer, Tuwaiq); co-op admin B2B MODERATE (Taawoni); Qudurat/Tahsili prep MODERATE in number of apps but WEAK in quality and brand; AI mock interviews and Saudi-specific career coaching WEAK; student-facing early-career marketplace (Handshake-style) WEAK.

### Inferences
- Large rounds (Baraya, Noon, Classera) cluster in capital-heavy or B2G models, so a small founder should avoid competing head-on there.
- Free government platforms (Jadarat, Doroob, Tamheer) raise the bar for paid job boards. Paid products should sit next to them, for example by preparing candidates to apply through them.

### Gaps
- Funding amounts for Braya, WTDcare and Healtec, and what Healtec does, could not be verified.
- No 2025 data was found on Bayt's KSA share, or on Altibbi and Sehhaty usage. These were not re-searched because the brief marks telehealth as known.
- No Saudi fitness-app or gym-market size data was found.

## Q3. Which proven Western models have no strong Saudi equivalent? (Concrete gaps)

### Takeaway
Six candidate gaps, ranked for a low-capital design and digital founder: (E1) an Arabic AI interview and Saudi-ATS CV coach; (E2) premium adaptive Qudurat/Tahsili prep; (H1) a family-caregiver support app for dementia and elderly care, with a later option of a non-medical companion marketplace; (H2) app-based guided physiotherapy (Hinge/Sword model) delivered with licensed physios. A student internship marketplace (E3) and culturally tailored chronic-disease coaching (H3) are secondary. H3 has more competition (Kilow) and more regulation.

### Cited Findings and gap cards

**H1. Family-caregiver support plus non-medical senior companion care**
- Western model: **Papa** (companions for seniors, paid by health plans) raised a $150M Series D in 2021 at a $1.4B valuation, $242M raised in total; 2021 revenue was $24M (CB Insights). In 2026 it is still running companion-care programmes for health plans. **Honor** (home-care network) raised a $140M Series D, and later reports cite a $70M Series E plus $300M debt. — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/senior-care-platform-papa-scoops-150m-series-d-funding); [CB Insights Papa](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/papa-technologies/financials); [Honor press](https://honorcare.com/news/press/homehealthcarenews-honor-raises-140m-to-fuel-national-expansion); [Mercom](https://mercomcapital.com/honor-secures-140-million-for-its-healthcare-booking-service/)
- Local problem and demand: women carry dementia care with high burden and unmet service needs, and awareness is low (see Q1). Families default to recruiting domestic workers through Musaned. — [PMC9957410](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9957410/); [Al Watan](https://www.alwatan.com.sa/article/1168173)
- Saudi/GCC competitors: none found for a caregiver app or a non-medical companion marketplace. Medical home care is served by Baraya, Braya, WTDcare and TruDoc (see Q2).
- Revenue model: a caregiver app could be freemium (care plans, shared family task board, Arabic dementia training courses, medication reminders), plus B2B2C sales to employers and insurers, plus referral fees to licensed home-care providers. A companion marketplace would take a 15–25% commission (typical marketplace take, unverified for KSA).
- Difficulty and capital: the app alone is low capital (design and dev only). A companion marketplace is moderate to high: it needs worker supply, vetting, insurance and trust.
- Regulation: anything clinical (nursing, IV, physio at home) needs an MOH facility licence through the Seha platform. Requirements include CBAHI accreditation, SCFHS-classified staff, a licensed medical director and SFDA-approved devices (per a consultancy guide; verify on Seha or business.sa). This is HEAVILY LICENSED, so stay non-medical or partner with licensed providers. Employing caregivers or domestic workers involves HRSD and Musaned labour rules. — [Rukn consultancy](https://rukn-services.com/%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%AE%D9%8A%D8%B5-%D8%AE%D8%AF%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%AA%D9%85%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%B6-%D9%85%D9%86%D8%B2%D9%84%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%86%D8%B4%D8%A7%D8%B7-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%AB/)

**H2. App-based guided physiotherapy and MSK care (Hinge Health / Sword Health model)**
- Western evidence: **Hinge Health** went public on the NYSE in May 2025 ($437M raised at $32 per share). 2024 revenue was about $390M (+33%); Q2 2025 revenue was $139M (+55% YoY); FY2025 is about $588M per Sacra (secondary). **Sword Health** (private) is valued at about $4.15B against a reported ~$240M run rate (unverified analysis). — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/digital-health/hinge-health-files-go-public-signaling-potential-ipo-revival-digital-health); [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/hinge-health-beats-revenue-expectations-first-public-earnings-q2-2025/756937/); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/hinge-health); [healthcare.digital](https://www.healthcare.digital/single-post/sword-health-the-clear-successor-to-the-digital-msk-throne)
- Local problem: physiotherapy is part of MOH home care, and private providers (PhysioHome/Ameen) are home-visit based, which is expensive and doesn't scale. Low activity among women means women-only, at-home guided programmes fit cultural preferences (inference).
- Competitors: PhysioHome/Ameen (seed), Physiowell (plans only), PhysioMantra (unverified). Assessment: WEAK.
- Revenue: B2C subscription or programme fee (e.g., an 8–12 week back, knee or postpartum programme), B2B sales to employers and insurers, and pay-per-session teleconsults with licensed physios.
- Difficulty: low to moderate if it starts as a content and exercise-tracking app with partnered licensed physiotherapists. Moderate to high if it adds sensors or AI diagnostic claims.
- Regulation: physiotherapists must be SCFHS-licensed. Telehealth delivery likely needs an MOH-licensed telehealth provider or partner. Diagnostic or treatment software claims may fall under SFDA medical-device (SaMD) rules, which I inferred and did not verify. The MOH Sandbox (2024) is a possible route.

**H3 (secondary). Culturally tailored chronic-disease and diabetes lifestyle coaching (Noom / Lark / Virta model)**
- Demand: diabetes affects about 18.7–23% of adults (conflicting sources), obesity is above 24%, and the digital diabetes market is about $145M (market research). — [IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/saudi-arabia-digital-diabetes-management-market)
- Competitors: Kilow ($2.5M seed, GLP-1 plus coaching), plus telehealth majors. Assessment: MODERATE. Possible angle: non-prescribing Arabic meal planning for traditional Saudi food and Ramadan, for diabetics.
- Western evidence: none gathered this round, so this is a gap.
- Regulation: prescribing needs licensed physicians (as Kilow uses). Pure nutrition coaching is lighter but should use SCFHS-licensed dietitians for credibility.

**E1. Arabic AI interview coach plus Saudi-ATS CV optimizer**
- Western model: AI mock-interview and CV tools (e.g., Big Interview, Final Round AI, Teal, Kickresume). **No funding or revenue evidence was gathered in this research round; treat Western proof as UNVERIFIED and gather it before relying on it.**
- Local demand: unemployment is 20.4% for Saudi women aged 15–24 and 9% for all Saudi women (Q1 2026). Jadarat channels large applicant volumes (114K job seekers in its pilot), so candidates compete for screened roles. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-unemployment-falls-to-64-in-q1-2026-1.500592445); [Gulf News Jadarat](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-launches-jadarat-employment-platform-with-over-70000-job-openings-1.103871823)
- Competitors: Astr, ATScv and Intelliview, all small, hackathon or solo projects with unverified claims. Assessment: WEAK.
- Revenue: freemium with paid packs (SAR 29–99 per month, an assumption), B2B licences for universities' career centers and Tamheer/HRDF training partners, and white-label for recruiters.
- Difficulty: LOW capital. It is LLM API-based, and a design-led Arabic UX is the differentiator. The risk is low barriers to entry: Bayt, LinkedIn or ChatGPT could add similar features.
- Regulation: light. Main constraint is the Saudi Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) for CVs and audio/video data (my inference; PDPL was not researched this round).

**E2. Premium adaptive Qudurat/Tahsili prep (Magoosh / UWorld / Khan-style)**
- Local demand: these tests are compulsory for Saudi university entry. A fragmented set of apps with low ratings shows demand with poor quality (Dar Al Harf: 81K+ downloads, 2.9 rating). — [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/dr-lhrf/1643227111); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/id6756188408)
- Competitors: many small apps plus offline training centers (e.g., "Tahaddi Al-Qudurat" center app). Noon Academy may cover this too (not verified). Assessment: MODERATE by count, WEAK by quality.
- Western evidence: none gathered this round (gap).
- Revenue: subscriptions per test season, diagnostic tests, school B2B.
- Difficulty: low to moderate. Producing a question bank is the main cost, and copyright of ETEC questions is a legal risk. Apps state they are "not affiliated with ETEC/Qiyas". — [GPA Pro listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6782892565)
- Regulation: light for digital content. Physical training centers need licensing (not researched).

**E3 (secondary). Student early-career marketplace (Handshake model)**
- Western evidence: Handshake's last priced round was a $200M Series F at a $3.5B valuation (2022). Its recruiting business is about $150–250M in revenue (secondary sources disagree). It is now pivoting to an AI data-labeling business of about $1B annualized gross revenue. — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/handshake-revenue-growth-valuation); [arr.club](https://www.arr.club/handshake/handshake-reaches-250m-arr)
- Local: compulsory co-op training plus youth unemployment. Taawoni already holds the B2B admin layer (14 universities). Firms run their own co-op programmes (McKinsey Maqdira, Strategy&). — [Waya](https://waya.media/saudi-edtech-taawoni-raises-usd-1-6m-to-streamline-training-programs/); [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/middle-east/the-maqdira-program)
- Assessment: MODERATE (Taawoni plus free Jadarat). Two-sided marketplace cold-start makes this harder for a solo founder.

### Inferences
- The best fit for limited capital and design skills is E1 and E2 (software only, light regulation), then H1 as a caregiver app (non-medical). H2 is attractive but needs licensed clinical partners from day one.
- Avoid as a solo founder: licensed medical home care (MOH facility licence plus CBAHI), GLP-1 prescribing, and K-12 tutoring (Noon and Classera are well funded).

### Gaps
- No revenue or funding evidence was gathered for Western mock-interview, CV, test-prep, Noom/Lark or caregiver-app companies (e.g., Final Round AI, Teal, Magoosh, Noom, CaringBridge). This needs a follow-up.
- MOH and SFDA rules for app-based physiotherapy and wellness apps (SaMD classification, telehealth licensing) were not confirmed from primary sources.
- No pricing benchmarks were found for Saudi consumers' willingness to pay for career or test-prep subscriptions.
- The Saudi PDPL implications were not researched.
