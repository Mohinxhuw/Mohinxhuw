## 1. How bad is commuting in Riyadh (and Jeddah/Dammam), and what has the Riyadh Metro changed?

### Takeaway
Riyadh is Saudi Arabia's most congested city. TomTom 2025 puts average congestion at 43.7% and rush-hour time lost at about 66 hours per driver per year. The evening peak is the worst part: a 10 km trip takes about 24 minutes. The Metro (opened Dec 2024) passed 100M riders in under 9 months, but getting to and from stations (first/last mile) and parking in the heat are still friction points. In June 2026 the government launched flexible start times in business districts, which shows officials see traffic as a productivity problem.

### Cited Findings
- Riyadh 2025: average congestion 43.7% (−2.2 pp vs 2024). A 10 km trip averages 16 min 48 s. Evening rush: 10 km in about 24 min 12 s at 90.4% congestion. Morning rush: about 17 min 55 s at 50.2%. Average rush-hour speed about 28.5 km/h. About 66 hours lost in rush hour in 2025 (about 5 h less than 2024). Worst day was Thursday 13 Feb (70%) — [TomTom Riyadh](https://www.tomtom.com/traffic-index/riyadh-traffic/); [TomTom 2026 headline numbers](https://www.tomtom.com/newsroom/explainers-and-insights/tomtom-traffic-index-2026-headline-numbers/)
- Jeddah 2025: congestion 27.2% (+1.9 pp), 43 h lost in rush hour (+5 h vs 2024), 10 km in about 15 min. The worst day, 27 Feb, peaked at 137% at 5pm — [TomTom Jeddah](https://www.tomtom.com/traffic-index/jeddah-traffic)
- Dammam 2025: congestion 28.8% (−1 pp), 44 h lost (−2.5 h), 10 km in 16 min 51 s — [TomTom Dammam](https://www.tomtom.com/traffic-index/city/dammam/); [TomTom Saudi country table](https://www.tomtom.com/traffic-index/saudi-arabia-country-traffic)
- Car dependence: the Riyadh mayor said over 90% of residents use their own cars. Arab News describes near-constant congestion caused by accidents, random parking, bottlenecks and weak public transport — [Arab News](https://arab.news/b336t)
- One-way commute in Riyadh is about 38 minutes (self-reported, undated crowd-sourced Numbeo figure) **[UNVERIFIED / low quality]**. I found no recent official household travel survey — [Numbeo via search summary](https://www.xmap.ai/blog/saudi-arabia-road-traffic-all-what-you-need-to-know)
- Riyadh Metro passed 100M cumulative passengers by late Aug 2025, less than 9 months after its Dec 2024 launch. Blue Line about 46.5M, Red about 17M, Orange about 12M. Punctuality 99.78% — [Gulf Business](https://gulfbusiness.com/en/2025/transport/riyadh-metro-surpasses-100-million-passengers-in-under-nine-months); [Arab News PK](https://www.arabnews.pk/saudi-arabia/riyadh-metro-surpasses-100m-passengers-in-under-9-months-2613047)
- TGA quarterly counts: about 23.6M (Q2 2025) and 25.2M (Q3 2025) Riyadh Metro passengers — [Argaam Q3](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1850907); [Argaam Q2](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1830525). Note: quarterly sums do not reconcile neatly with the 100M cumulative milestone, so counting methods likely differ.
- Rider pain: "The most challenging aspect has been getting to the station and back home." Parking is hard unless you arrive early, and riders walk from parking to station in the heat — [Arab News, "Riyadh's coolest ride this summer is the metro"](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2607272/amp)
- Plans announced: 19 park-and-ride lots at terminal stations, up to 120 on-demand minibuses for first/last mile, and free 1–5 km first/last-mile trips planned from Feb 2025. I could not confirm full rollout — [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1775724)
- Employers are responsible for station-to-office transport under Labour Law Art. 61 since Feb 2025 **[UNVERIFIED — vendor claim by Swvl]** — [Swvl blog](https://www.swvl.com/blog/last-mile-transport-riyadh-metro-employer-guide)
- From 2 June 2026, RCRC and MHRSD introduced a 4-hour flexible attendance window for more than 50 government entities in six zones (KAFD, Digital City, Diplomatic Quarter, Laysen Valley, Granada Business, Roshn Front). Civil-service staff can start 5:30–9:30am and Labour Law entities 7–11am. The stated goal is spreading morning traffic — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2645894/saudi-arabia); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/world%2Fgulf%2Fsaudi%2Fsaudi-arabia-launches-flexible-working-hours-across-riyadh-business-districts-to-ease-congestion-1.500561742); [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/local/na/2251350)

### Inferences
- About 66 hours a year lost only to rush-hour delay (on top of normal driving time), plus a 24-minute evening 10 km trip, means that every errand needing a cross-city drive after work costs roughly an hour round trip. This is the main reason "do it for me / bring it to me" services exist.
- The Metro moves the pain rather than removing it. First/last mile, parking and walking in the heat are now the friction points. Openings: B2B commuter-shuttle coordination for SMEs, or a "metro + last-mile" trip planner/aggregator. Both are risky for a solo founder because Swvl, Careem and RCRC's own feeders already play here.
- Flexible start times create new "time windows" (early-morning starters with free afternoons). Errand services could sell booking slots around these windows.

### Gaps
- No official 2024–2026 survey of average door-to-door commute times in Riyadh/Jeddah/Dammam was found. The 2007 Bayt survey (49 min daily in Riyadh) is too old to use.
- No full-year 2025 Metro ridership from RCRC. No data on whether the on-demand feeder buses reached full scale.
- I could not access Reddit r/saudiarabia threads (search returned none). Social-media frustration evidence is therefore mostly from Arab News anecdotes.

## 2. Which errands still need physical presence or waiting despite digital government, and is the mu'aqqib (معقب) market still active and how is it priced?

### Takeaway
Most routine transactions are digital (Absher, Najiz, Tawakkalna). Several still need an in-person appointment: biometrics, some passport/iqama services, driving licence issue/renewal, vehicle registration and ownership transfer, plus the yearly vehicle inspection (فحص دوري). The mu'aqqib trade is alive and has moved online: offices advertise "remote" services across the Kingdom. Pricing is opaque and per transaction. Ad prices I found are around SAR 499–550 per package **[UNVERIFIED]**. Most demand probably comes from small businesses (CR, Qiwa, visas) rather than salaried individuals.

### Cited Findings
- Absher appointments exist for services "that cannot be completed remotely" because they need personal verification, fingerprints or original documents. Examples: passport issue (some cases), biometrics, in-person iqama services. Traffic (المرور): licence issue/renewal, vehicle registration, ownership transfer. Standard passport renewal is fully online — [wdeftksa guide, Absher appointments 2026](https://www.wdeftksa.com/sa/guides/absher-appointments) **[commercial guide, not official]**; [passport guide](https://www.wdeftksa.com/sa/guides/passport-appointment-renewal)
- Absher allowed passport issue/renewal without first paying traffic fines — [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/people-situations/na/2262887)
- SASO's 2025 report counted 6,065,516 periodic vehicle-inspection operations and 19 new sites, with coverage reaching 85% of the population within 80 km. The system moved from "semi-random" walk-ins to appointments after heavy congestion at centres — [Al-Madina](https://www.al-madina.com/ampArticle/908165); [Al-Muraba](https://www.almuraba.net/?p=868181)
- Mu'aqqib ads on Mstaml: "تعقيب وخدمات جميع المناطق" (follow-up services, all regions) at SAR 550 and SAR 499, undated **[UNVERIFIED single-seller listings]** — [Mstaml listing](https://www.mstaml.com/product/%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%82%D9%8A%D8%A8-%D9%88%D8%AE%D8%AF%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%A9); [Mstaml Riyadh](https://www.mstaml.com/sa/product/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%A9/%D9%85%D9%86%D8%B7%D9%82%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%B6/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%B6/%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%82%D9%8A%D8%A8-%D8%AE%D8%AF%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%A9/%D8%AA%D8%B9%D9%82%D9%8A%D8%A8-%D9%88%D8%AE%D8%AF%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%A9)
- Commercial articles on "mu'aqqib offices in Riyadh 2026" exist. One claims that 2023 Ministry of Commerce rules require mu'aqqibs to hold a licence and register in a unified government-services system **[UNVERIFIED; could not open the page]** — [rukn-services](https://rukn-services.com/makatib-taqib-riyadh-2026/)
- Search found no Saudi app branded as an online mu'aqqib marketplace. Offers are scattered across classifieds (Mstaml, OpenSooq) — [OpenSooq](https://sa.opensooq.com/en/search/281268830)

### Inferences
- For salaried Saudi professionals, the remaining in-person errands are infrequent but painful: licence renewal, car transfer after buying or selling, inspection, Najm/accident follow-ups, and expat family iqama/biometrics. Many expat professionals already pay their company's PRO or a mu'aqqib for family visas.
- The pain is less "it must be done in person" and more "I don't know the exact steps, documents and fees, and I lose half a day getting it wrong." Some of it can be solved digitally.
- Business ideas:
  1. **"دليل المعاملة" (transaction guide): a checklist and booking-reminder web product.** A verified, current, Arabic step-by-step guide for about 50 top life transactions (buying or selling a car, new baby, marriage, adding dependants, licence renewal). It would list documents, fees, which platform to use, whether presence is needed, and give a calendar reminder for expiry dates (licence, istimara, inspection, iqama). Monetisation: freemium plus affiliate links (inspection booking, insurance) plus a paid "do it for me" handoff. SEO-led and low capital.
  2. **Vetted mu'aqqib/errand marketplace (light service).** Fixed-price menu (e.g., "car ownership transfer + inspection, SAR X"), escrow via payment link, ratings, WhatsApp-first. It would fix the current opacity and trust gap in classifieds. Risk: licensing rules and the fact that individuals rarely need it, so start with SMEs and expat families.

### Gaps
- No reliable price table for mu'aqqib fees by transaction type (licence, vehicle transfer, visas). The figures above are single ads.
- No data on the number of licensed mu'aqqib offices, or on whether the 2023 regulation exists as described.
- No official list of services that still require in-person attendance. No data on Absher appointment wait times.
- I did not cover Najm (accident reports) or Taqdeer (vehicle damage assessment) presence requirements. Worth a targeted search.

## 3. What concierge, personal-assistant and errand services exist, and how are they rated?

### Takeaway
Saudi Arabia has a dense on-demand app layer (food and grocery delivery, Mrsool-style "fetch anything", mobile car wash, cleaning, home maintenance). I found **no established general personal-concierge or errand-running brand for professionals** in Riyadh. Comparable models exist in Dubai (Mashaweer, Wheely concierge). Users already pay for convenience but complain about inflated prices and dependence.

### Cited Findings
- Arab News (May 2026): apps now cover food, groceries, car washing, cleaning and personal care on demand. Traffic congestion is cited as a driver of time-saving services. Internet penetration is above 99% (DataReportal 2026). Interviewees say prices feel inflated and that sellers "exploit" dependence — [Arab News PK](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2643426) (via search summary; direct fetch failed)
- Lalamove Riyadh positions itself for personal errands and parcels (send or collect items across districts). It is a courier, not a concierge — [Lalamove](https://www.lalamove.com/en-sa/blog/which-delivery-app-is-right-for-you-in-riyadh)
- Dubai's Mashaweer is an errand startup charging per trip rather than per hour — [Entrepreneur ME](https://mena.entrepreneur.com/en-ae/entrepreneurs/startup-mashaweer-wants-to-do-your-errands-in-dubai/240028). Wheely concierge is invite-only in Dubai, not in Riyadh — [Wheely](https://dev-site.wheely.com/en/classes/bouquet)
- Home maintenance apps: YellaFix (Muheel/ALJ) covers Riyadh and Jeddah (electrical, plumbing, AC, pest control, cleaning) with Dammam planned — [ALJ](https://alj.com/en/?p=87279). The Fixit app has too few ratings to show a score — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/qa/app/fixit-app/id6748888970). "M Manager / مدير الصيانة" offers per-technician ratings and up to a 30-day warranty — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6446023128)
- Mobile car wash: the Ghasla (غسلة) app starts at about SAR 180 per package. Mobile wash typically costs SAR 20–40 more than a fixed car wash. Monthly bundles such as 4 washes for SAR 220 exist **[commercial/blog sources]** — [Al-Muraba](https://www.almuraba.net/?p=486818); [Syarah guide](https://syarah.com/carsguide/cheapest-car-wash/); W5 app — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/w5-%D8%AF%D8%A8%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%88-%D9%81%D8%A7%D9%8A%D9%81/id1618609911)
- Household help is a big existing spend. Musaned recruitment-fee caps (Jan 2024, excl. VAT): Philippines SAR 14,700, Sri Lanka 13,800, Bangladesh 11,750, Kenya 9,000, Uganda 8,300, Ethiopia 5,900. Mandatory 2-year contract insurance since Feb 2024 — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-cuts-domestic-worker-hiring-fee-from-philippines-sri-lanka-bangladesh-uganda-kenya-and-ethiopia-1.100458587); [Migrant-Rights](https://www.migrant-rights.org/2024/01/saudi-arabia-introduces-mandatory-contract-insurance-and-other-initiatives-for-domestic-worker-recruitment/). June 2026 averages incl. VAT: Philippines SAR 14,299, Sri Lanka 13,821, Bangladesh 7,097, Ethiopia 3,946 — [ksaexpats](https://ksaexpats.com/?p=51634)

### Inferences
- Willingness to pay is clearly shown. Households pay SAR 4k–14k just to recruit a domestic worker (before salary), around SAR 180+ for at-home car-wash bundles, and delivery fees routinely. The unsolved part is **coordination**: someone who books, chases, waits for technicians and gives one point of contact. The data does not prove a fully staffed concierge works at small scale; Dubai examples suggest a niche, premium market.
- Business ideas:
  1. **"Home ops" subscription for dual-income families (light service + WhatsApp + simple web dashboard).** Monthly fee (e.g., SAR 199–399, **hypothetical pricing**) to coordinate AC servicing, car wash and inspection, appliance repair, pest control and document renewals using existing providers. The founder adds the schedule, reminders and vendor vetting. Start with one neighbourhood in Riyadh (e.g., north Riyadh compounds).
  2. **Household calendar / "home passport" app (digital product).** Tracks AC filter changes, istimara/licence/iqama expiries, domestic-worker contract dates, car service intervals, warranties, and sends reminders plus one-tap booking links to partners (affiliate revenue). Low capital and suited to design skills.

### Gaps
- No app-store rating data was collected for Mrsool, HungerStation, Ghasla, YellaFix or Sanaeyi. Rating counts and common complaints are still needed.
- No Saudi concierge or virtual-assistant service was found at all. That could mean it is absent or that my search missed it (try Instagram/Khamsat searches for "مساعد شخصي" (personal assistant) or "خدمة مشاوير" (errand-run service)).
- No Khamsat or Haraj price samples for errand-running ("مشاوير") were collected.

## 4. Heat-related pains: outdoor activity, car care, AC maintenance demand in summer

### Takeaway
Summer heat (routinely above 40°C in Riyadh, above 50°C possible in the Gulf) is codified in policy: a midday outdoor work ban runs from 15 June to 15 September. It drives AC repair overload, mobile car care, and the move from outdoor to indoor or early/late activity. It also makes every walk to a car, metro station or office a friction point. AC maintenance is reactive ("people wait until it breaks"), which leaves room for preventive subscriptions.

### Cited Findings
- MHRSD 2025 midday outdoor work ban: 12:00–15:00 for all private-sector establishments, 15 June to 15 Sept 2025. 94% compliance was reported — [People Matters ME](https://me.peoplemattersglobal.com/news/wellbeing/saudi-arabia-enforces-midday-outdoor-work-ban-until-september-15-50323); [DLA Piper 2026](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/saudi-arabia-enforces-seasonal-midday-work-ban-to-protect-workers). About 1,910 violations between 15 Jun and 26 Aug (year not explicit) — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-detects-nearly-2000-violations-of-midday-work-ban-during-summer-inspections-1.500254271)
- Riyadh temperatures "clear 40°C on a daily basis" in summer, and summer is the most stressful time for AC repair crews. A company owner says most customers don't request maintenance until there is a problem, sometimes leading to serious damage (AFP, Sept 2023) — [Al-Monitor/AFP](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2023/09/no-time-rest-ac-repairmen-sweltering-saudi)
- Riyadh routine AC maintenance costs about SAR 200–500 per visit, and major repairs can exceed SAR 2,000 **[commercial guide, UNVERIFIED]** — [IPS News HVAC guide, May 2025](https://ipsnews.net/business/2025/05/06/top-hvac-companies-in-riyadh-a-complete-guide-to-heating-cooling-experts/)
- Saudi AC market: USD 2.14B (2024), forecast USD 2.88B by 2030 (market-research estimate). A SAR 900 discount is available per high-efficiency AC, up to 6 units, for citizens — search summary of [Kuwait Times/AFP](https://kuwaittimes.com/no-time-to-rest-for-ac-workers-in-sweltering-saudi/) and market reports **[UNVERIFIED]**
- Metro riders describe walking from parking to the station in the heat, and the metro is pitched as "Riyadh's coolest ride this summer" — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2607272/amp)
- Diriyah is installing outdoor air-conditioning to bring plaza temperatures to about 28°C in summer, which shows how much outdoor activity depends on cooling — search summary (Dec 2025 report; primary URL not captured) **[UNVERIFIED source link]**
- Physical activity (GASTAT): 58.5% of adults met the 150 min/week target in 2024 (men 64%, women 46.3%; Saudis 54.1% vs non-Saudis 62.5%). In 2025 the figure was 59.1% (men 66.5%, women 43.1%) — [GASTAT 2024 PDF](https://stats.gov.sa/sites/default/files/Physical%20Activity%20Statistics%20Publication%202024%20EN.pdf); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-585-of-adults-active-but-youth-sedentary-habits-on-the-rise-1.1733140777697). Note: some outlets wrongly report youth figures (23.2% male / 14% female) as adult figures.

### Inferences
- Roughly 4 in 10 adults, and more than half of women, don't meet activity guidelines. Heat plus long hours plus commute likely explains part of this (inference, not proven causally). Ideas: an **at-home or online coaching product in Arabic** built around 20–30 minute indoor sessions that fit after commute; or a **"cool hours" fitness/activity finder** listing indoor and AC'd venues, women-only slots, and early-morning or late-night options by district.
- AC: reactive behaviour plus summer overload means that a **preventive AC-care subscription broker** (book pre-summer servicing in March–May at a locked price, with guaranteed priority in July) solves a real timing pain. A solo founder can run it as a booking layer over vetted technicians.
- Car care in heat (sun damage, dust, batteries failing in heat): mobile wash already exists, so the gap is bundling (wash + battery check + tint/ceramic + inspection reminder) rather than a new wash app.

### Gaps
- No 2025/2026 Riyadh temperature records or days above 45°C from NCM (National Center for Meteorology). Pull these from NCM for exact numbers.
- No measured data on summer spikes in AC-repair demand or price surges (only anecdote).
- No data on summer car-battery or tyre failures, or on gym membership costs and usage by time of day.

## 5. Long working hours and household-management load (who suffers)

### Takeaway
Saudi employees usually work about 46 hours a week (GASTAT via Trading Economics, Q4 2025), and 45.8% worked more than 40 hours in 2023. Add 1–1.5 hours of daily commuting in Riyadh, and evening errands collide with the worst traffic peak (evening rush). The groups hit hardest are likely dual-income Saudi families (female labour participation has grown), expat professionals managing iqama and family paperwork, and anyone without a driver.

### Cited Findings
- Usual weekly hours: 46 in Q3 and Q4 2025, averaging 45.7 from 2021 to 2025 (GASTAT series via aggregator) — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/saudi-arabia/average-weekly-hours)
- 45.8% of employees worked more than 40 hours/week in 2023. Men average 8.1 h/day and women 8.0 h/day — [HR Observer](https://www.thehrobserver.com/?p=1727); [Siasat](https://www.siasat.com/saudi-arabia-over-45-of-employees-worked-more-than-40-hours-a-week-in-2023-2946921/amp/)
- Legal ceiling: 8 h/day and 48 h/week remain after the 2024 Labour Law amendments (effective 2025) — [ksaexpats](https://ksaexpats.com/working-hours-saudi-arabia-expats/)
- Evening peak in Riyadh is 90.4% congestion (24 min per 10 km) — [TomTom](https://www.tomtom.com/traffic-index/riyadh-traffic/)

### Inferences
- A 46-hour week plus roughly an hour of commuting leaves weekday evenings almost entirely in the worst traffic. Errands get pushed to weekends (Fri/Sat), which concentrates demand for appointments, car services and repairs. A weekend-slot booking product or "we do it on weekdays while you work" positioning fits this.
- The most bankable segment for a solo founder is likely **dual-income families in north Riyadh plus expat professionals**, because they have the highest coordination load and higher incomes. This segment is inferred, not measured.

### Gaps
- No GASTAT time-use survey (time spent on housework, care, errands) was found for 2024–2026. Check whether GASTAT has published one.
- No survey data on willingness to pay for errand or concierge services in KSA.
- No data on the cost of private drivers (domestic driver recruitment and salary) in 2025–2026. Musaned covers drivers too, but driver-specific prices weren't retrieved.
